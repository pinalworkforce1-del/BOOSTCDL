A CDL gets you qualified to operate the vehicle. Employers are also looking for drivers they can trust with safety, communication, freight, customers, and the entire delivery process.

Employer-demand overview.
Recent employer demand shows just how much professional driving is changing. Truck driving, Class A CDL requirements, and commercial driver’s licenses remain central, but employers are also asking for communication, strong driving records, loading and unloading, customer service, vehicle inspections, professionalism, operations, and safety. Communication appeared in more than seven hundred recent postings. Customer service appeared in more than six hundred. Pre-trip and post-trip inspection skills appeared in nearly five hundred. Operations demand grew more than two hundred percent, and safety standards doubled. The message is clear: the license matters, but employers also need drivers they can trust to operate safely, communicate professionally, manage the work, and take responsibility for the vehicle, freight, and customer experience.
The license gets you into the cab. Professional judgment keeps the work moving safely. Employers are asking for drivers who can operate equipment, communicate, manage delivery responsibilities, protect freight, and represent the company professionally.

Source: Lightcast Q3 2026 • CDL-related job postings • Jun 2025–Jul 2026. Counts shown are anchor skills from the employer-demand extract provided for this build.
